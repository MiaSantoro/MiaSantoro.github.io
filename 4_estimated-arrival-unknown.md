# Estimated Arrival : Unknown
12/07/2026

**Ladies and gentlemen,please remain seated until your identity has fully formed.**

**We apologise for the delay.**

**Current estimates suggest completion may take anywhere between several years and an entire lifetime.** 

**Thank you for your patience.**

&emsp;

Ridiculous,isn't it?

And yet most of us spend an alarming amount of time believing exactly that.

I have spent an embarrassing amount of time believing that my next life was just one plane ticket away.

Not literally,of course.I wasn't expecting passport control to hand me a different personality.

But I did quietly assume that somewhere between a new city,a new language and enough distance,I might finally become the version of myself I'd been trying to catch for years.

Looking back,it's a surprisingly common fantasy.We rarely move just because we want a different place.

More often,we hope the person living there might turn out to be someone we'd rather be.

A few days ago,I was talking about this with my friend Suz.Somewhere between discussing our plans and pretending we had one,we realised how exhausting it is to feel that you must eventually *become someone*.

**Not simply exist.Become someone.**

As if life were slowly building towards a final reveal.

*"There you go. That's who you've been all along."*

It's a comforting story.I'm just no longer convinced it's true.

The previous chapter left me with an uncomfortable suspicion.
If entities emerge through relationships...if forests,cities and organisms become coherent not because something mysterious sits inside them,but because countless interactions briefly hold them together...

...what happens if I stop making an exception for people?For me?

I wasn't trying to write about identity.I was simply curious to see what would happen if I took the previous chapter seriously.Slightly irresponsible,I guess.

The more I thought about emergence,the less convincing identity became.Not because it disappeared.

Because it behaved suspiciously like everything else I had been studying.

It wasn't sitting there.It was happening.

For years I imagined identity as something hidden somewhere inside me.Something I simply hadn't managed to discover yet.

Well apparently,we've built an entire culture around this idea.

*Find yourself.*

*Be authentic.*

As though somewhere beneath enough mistakes,enough travelling,enough heartbreak,enough therapy and quite possibly,enough expensive coffee,there is a perfectly finished person patiently waiting to be uncovered.How considerate of them.

The strange thing is that we don't just believe this story.We organise entire lives around it.

Education asks us who we want to become.Careers reward consistency.Politics increasingly demands clear identities, clear borders and clear sides,as though ambiguity were a software bug waiting to be fixed.

Reality,meanwhile,keeps displaying a remarkable lack of cooperation.

The more I look around,the harder it becomes to believe that anything exists in complete isolation.

Not forests.Not cities.Not artworks.

And,increasingly,not ourselves.

Of course,**something must persist.**

Otherwise,this paragraph would have to admit it was written by several different people.That seems unnecessarily complicated.

But perhaps persistence isn't the same thing as permanence.Perhaps identity isn't something we possess.Perhaps it's a relatively stable pattern continuously produced by relationships.

**Stable enough that other people recognise us.**

**Never stable enough to stop changing.**

Maybe that's why I've started to distrust the question *Who am I?*

Not because it's meaningless.Because it quietly assumes there's a final answer.

A finished version.

A completed object.

A point at which becoming politely ends.

Instead of asking,

*Who am I?*

perhaps we should have been asking,

**Why am I so desperate to stop changing?**
