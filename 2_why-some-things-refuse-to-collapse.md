# Why some things refuse to collapse

Let's talk about something I find kinda puzzling.

**Once something exists,how does it manage to keep going?**

I keep coming back to this question.It shows up in the studio when a structure I expected to fail somehow stays together.It appears in damaged materials that refuse to become useless,in ecosystems that survive despite continuous disturbance and occasionally in the pile of objects I've convinced myself I'll organise tomorrow.

At first glance persistence seems almost boring.

Buildings remain standing,cities continue to function,organisms survive,ecosystems endure.We tend to assume that things simply keep existing until something dramatic interrupts them.The more I paid attention,however,the more the opposite seemed true.Very little survives because it is perfectly stable.

**Most things survive because they are constantly adapting,repairing themselves,losing parts,improvising and finding new ways to continue.**

Persistence does not coincide with stability.Actually I have started wondering whether stability is sometimes overrated.
A stable system is one that does not change.A persistent system,on the other hand,may be changing all the time.It may lose components,alter its structure,pass through moments of crisis and still somehow remain recognisably itself.

**Persistence begins to look less like a condition and more like a negotiation.**

Biology offers endless examples.The human body feels permanent,yet many of the cells that compose it are continuously replaced.Tissues regenerate,neural connections reorganise themselves and the organism constantly responds to both internal and external changes.*Identity does not depend on keeping the same matter forever.*

What persists is not the matter.What persists is the relationship.

I suspect this is also why damaged materials keep attracting me.Not because they symbolise failure but because they make visible the invisible work required to continue existing.

The same principle appears within ecosystems.For a long time ecological thinking imagined nature as something moving towards equilibrium.More recent perspectives suggest something far less...tidy.Wildfires,droughts,migrations,climate fluctuations and invasive species constantly reshape environments.The remarkable part is not that change happens.The remarkable part is that *some systems keep going anyway.*

Ecologists often describe this through the concept of resilience.I like this idea,although I think resilience is sometimes misunderstood.**It does not mean avoiding transformation.It means surviving through transformation.**

Perhaps what makes a system interesting is not its perfection but its *vulnerability.*
Perfectly closed systems often turn out to be surprisingly fragile.Faced with unexpected events,they have little room to adapt.Complex systems,-by contrast-frequently survive because they contain redundancies,imperfections,mistakes and unfinished conditions.

Which is comforting for me,considering how many mistakes end up becoming part of my own process.

This observation extends far beyond biology.Cities,for example,appear solid and permanent but they depend on countless invisible infrastructures: electrical grids,water systems,transport networks,digital communications,logistics and information flows.Most of the time we don't notice any of this.*Until something stops working.Infrastructure becomes visible precisely at the moment of failure.*

The same could probably be said of *relationships.* They rarely survive because they remain perfectly balanced.More often,they persist because **they continuously renegotiate themselves through misunderstanding,adaptation,conflict,repair and change.**

In that sense,**fragility may not be the opposite of duration.It may be one of its conditions.**

Sometimes I wonder whether persistence is simply collapse happening more slowly.

This idea has changed the way I think about artistic practice.For centuries in the past,artworks were often associated with permanence,integrity and preservation.

***When I'm no longer here,this will still be here.Despite everything.Despite time.***

Durable materials once promised resistance against time.And the artwork was an immortal creature.Just think of *The Picture of Dorian Gray*.Wilde uses the story to explore the uneasy **relationship between art and life.** While Dorian attempts to turn his own existence into something permanently beautiful,untouched by age or consequence,the portrait quietly absorbs everything he tries to escape.The painting changes.He doesn't.

For a long time,**we liked to imagine that art could outlive us precisely because it remained unchanged.** I'm not so sure anymore.

Much of contemporary artistic practice seems to move in another direction.Deterioration,instability,incompleteness and transformation are no longer failures to avoid but conditions to work with.

**The artwork does not resist time;it negotiates with it.** And maybe that's the point.

I'm not particularly interested in watching things fall apart.

I'm fascinated by the moment they somehow *refuse to.*

*By repaired objects.*

*Residual materials.*

*Obsolete infrastructures.*

*Compromised ecologies.*

*Vulnerable bodies.*

What connects these examples **is not the absence of damage but their remarkable ability to keep negotiating with it.** At this point Persistence becomes less about resisting change and more about learning how to continue through it.

And perhaps this idea reaches beyond art,biology or technology.Perhaps this is simply **what life does.**

**It keeps going anyway.**

From here,the questions become almost aesthetic.

How can temporary equilibrium be represented?

How can relationships be made visible without pretending they are harmonious?

How can conflict become part of form rather than merely interrupting it?

As I keep following these questions,another distinction begins to feel increasingly unstable.

If persistence depends on relationships rather than on the nature of the elements involved,perhaps *the boundary between the living and the non-living is less solid than it appears.*

Or perhaps it never was.

Maybe that is why some things refuse to collapse in the first place.

**And maybe that is why,despite everything - despite failure,despite damage,despite time - some traces remain long after the structures that produced them have disappeared.**
