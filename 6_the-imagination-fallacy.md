# The Imagination Fallacy
15/08/2026

Has anyone ever considered the possibility that **imagination might be slightly overrated?**

Before I'm publicly exiled from the art world,let me explain.

Imagination is extraordinary.It gave us cathedrals,quantum mechanics,cinema and unfortunately,artist statements.I'm not arguing against it.It's just I'm beginning to suspect we've misunderstood what it's actually good at.

For the past few months I've been reading cognitive neuroscience with the touching optimism of someone who believes understanding a mechanism might prevent her from being manipulated by it.So far,the mechanism remains unimpressed.One idea,however,has refused to leave me alone.

**The brain doesn't simply perceive the world.It predicts it.**

Reality arrives a fraction too late so the brain fills in the gap with educated guesses.Most of the time that's an excellent strategy.If I imagine the tiger before confirming the tiger,evolution applauds.If I imagine a failed exhibition before making the work, evolution probably applauds too.My artistic practice,unfortunately,does not.

I am,more often than I would like to admit,stuck in what I'd call an *artistic paralysis.* That well known moment for at least every artist,the sadly famous art block.

*"What should I do now?What should I do next?"*

**But first af all what is an artistic paralysis?Or what is not?**

I came to the realization that an artistic paralysis is not a failure of imagination.Actually it's what happens when imagination becomes too successful.When you have too much imagination.Cause at that point the brain does something funny.And here I'm not just talking about an overthinking habit.

It doesn't merely imagine the artwork.

It imagines every possible version of it.

The successful one.

The embarrassing one.

The derivative one.

The misunderstood one.

The one critics secretly hate.

The one I eventually hate.

The yellow one and the one made in black.

Still all these versions don't really hold the truth,cause well well they don't really exist outside of my very busy brain cortex.

By the time I finally arrive in the studio **my imagination has already completed a retrospective.**
Which makes me wonder whether we've been flattering imagination for the wrong reasons.

We celebrate it because **it generates possibilities.**

But possibilities are politically neutral.The same imagination that invents an artwork can just as easily invent a reason not to make it.In fact,it often seems faster at the second task.

**The real paradox is that the very imagination we celebrate for producing art may be the thing that prevents it from coming into existence.**

This isn't a complaint.It's a hypothesis.

**Maybe imagination didn't evolve to create.**

Maybe it evolved to simulate.To rehearse futures before risking them.To anticipate consequences before paying for them.*Creating art may simply be one of the strangest things we've ever asked this system to do.*

Controversial to say **I don't think imagination is designed for art.**

I think art hijacks imagination for purposes it was never particularly interested in.Which would explain a great deal.

The more vividly I can imagine the future,the easier it becomes to mistake simulation for evidence.

**Imagined works have an unfair advantage.They never have to become real.** They never disappoint.They never collapse under gravity.They never meet an audience.They never have an opening where someone says *"Interesting..."* with the emotional warmth of an automatic email.

**Reality is much less elegant.** Materials resist.Ideas become ordinary.Wood bends.Glue dries too quickly.Language refuses to cooperate.

Reality has terrible manners.*But reality has something imagination will never possess.*

*It answers back.*

Perhaps that's the real difference between science and art.

**Science tries to reduce prediction error.**

**Art seems strangely committed to producing it.**

A scientific model becomes stronger when its predictions become more accurate.An artwork often becomes stronger when its predictions fail.The accident survives.The material disagrees.And the work slowly becomes something neither the artist nor the brain could have anticipated.

Maybe at this point **artists are not more imaginative than everyone else.** Maybe **they're simply worse at believing their own imagination.**

I find that strangely comforting.It means **my imagination isn't my enemy.It's just overqualified.** Useful.Brilliant.Occasionally insufferable.

*But not evidence.*

The question I've actually been trying to answer all along is

Not *What is art?*

But **what kind of brain produces art?**

Not a fearless one.Not a perfectly creative one.Perhaps simply a brain willing to discover that its own predictions are incomplete.

So I have arrived at a modest hypothesis.Perhaps courage is not the absence of fear.Perhaps courage is methodological.

It is the decision to **treat your own predictions as hypotheses rather than conclusions.**

To stop citing imagination as evidence.To allow reality the opportunity to interrupt.

**Reality has many flaws.**

It is inefficient.Untidy.Occasionally cruel.But it possesses one quality that no simulation,however sophisticated,will ever have.

It can surprise you.

And for an artist,surprise is not a side effect of the process.It is the process.

So tomorrow I'll probably walk back into the studio-space carrying exactly the same predictions.My brain will continue to insist it already knows how the story ends.The difference is that,lately,I've stopped mistaking confidence for accuracy.

My brain is welcome to continue generating predictions.

It has earned that privilege.

Mine is to discover which of them survive contact with reality.

**I'll make the work anyway.**
