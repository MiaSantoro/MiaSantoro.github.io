# Version 4.3 of the Truth
19/07/2026

I did a lot of writing about *uncertainty.*

This is convenient because **I genuinely have no idea what I am doing.** Unfortunately well,at some point,you still have to explain it.

The slime mold died.Again.

This was not part of the project.I forgot to feed it.

I could probably make this sound better.I could say that its death became an investigation into biological time,maintenance and the limits of control.Which is true.But it died because I forgot to feed it.I mean that is also true.And it happened first.

I bought the slime mold.I gave it somewhere to live.I controlled the food,the temperature,the light.

**I was,by every reasonable definition,in charge.**

Except the thing I was in charge of kept doing things without consulting me.It grew at the wrong time.Stopped at the wrong time.Went in the wrong direction.Wrong,obviously,meaning wrong for me.

The slime mold had no idea there was a plan.It had never seen the proposal.It was completely unaware of the conceptual framework.

So I adjusted.I moved the food.Changed the conditions.Waited.It reacted.I reacted to the reaction.This continued for a while.

Eventually I looked at the result and called it my work.

**My work** 

That sentence has been bothering me.

Because this doesn't only happen with living things.A material breaks and I use the broken version.A space doesn't allow what I planned,so the work becomes something else.I can't afford something so I find something cheaper and later realise it was better.

**Then someone asks me why.**

And I answer.

I am usually very convincing.I have learned to be.

Being an artist also means learning how to explain yourself professionally.

You have to know **why** you chose that material.

**Why** it is that size.

**Why** it is there.

**Why** you made it now and not three years ago.

**You need to know what it means,where it came from and where it is going.**

Preferably in 500 words.Sometimes 250.

You learn to make uncertainty sound precise.

You spend three weeks not knowing what you are doing and five minutes explaining exactly why you did it.The five minutes are the professional part.And the strange thing is that the explanation is often true.I just didn't know it at the time.

There is a very clean model of artistic production:intention,execution,outcome.

First the artist thinks.
Then the artist acts.
Then the artwork appears.

**Authorship** is easy to locate in this model.Reality is less organised.

In practice,intention can arrive late.Meaning can emerge through the encounter with a material rather than being imposed on it beforehand.A constraint can become a method.An accident can change the conceptual structure of the work entirely.

This does not remove authorship.But it makes it harder to say exactly **where authorship begins.**

Personally I have almost never started with a complete idea.Usually,I have something closer to a suspicion.Then I touch something.Something goes wrong.I keep the wrong thing.Months later,I understand **why.**

This is less impressive to explain in a portfolio.

*“I had a suspicion and then some stuff happened”* is apparently not a methodology.

So the timeline gets cleaned up.Not necessarily because we are lying.But because **we are expected to make sense.**

*You learn to say why.
Then how.
Then when.*

Eventually,you can make a process that was completely uncertain sound inevitable.That is a skill.I am just not sure what kind.

**Sometimes the work knows something before I do.** I don't mean that poetically.I mean that I physically make a decision I cannot yet explain.I repeat it.I notice it.Only later do I understand what I have been repeating.

**The thought arrives after the evidence.**

There is a name for this in research:**knowledge does not always precede practice.** Sometimes knowledge is produced through practice.In an artist statement,however,it is generally preferable to pretend you knew this from the beginning.

Maybe that is why I keep working with things I cannot completely control.Not because I am interested in losing control.That sounds too good.I just need something in the room that can disagree with me.

The slime mold was particularly good at this.No brain.No intention.No respect for the project.Still,somehow,it kept forcing me to change my mind.

*Then it died.* I forgot to feed it.

And for once,**I don't want to turn that into anything.**

It wasn't a collaboration.

It wasn't the material asserting its agency.

It wasn't a beautiful failure.

**I killed the slime mold.**

Then I made work about what it taught me.

I am still trying to decide which of those two things is worse.
